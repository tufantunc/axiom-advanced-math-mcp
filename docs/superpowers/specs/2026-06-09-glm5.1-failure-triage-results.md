# GLM-5.1 Failure Triage — Results

**Date:** 2026-06-09
**Branch:** `glm5-failure-triage` (isolated worktree)
**Inputs:** pre-Tier-1/2/3 traces — `2026-05-08-08-42-51-zai-cas-quick-details.jsonl` (CAS, 60 problems, 19 failures) and `2026-05-08-13-08-05-zai-olympiad-quick-details.jsonl` (Omni-MATH ≥7, 50 problems, 49 failures).
**Method:** two analysis subagents classified every `toolAugmented.correct === false` record; the controller **spot-checked the raw traces** and corrected the subagents' (optimistic) tier attribution.

## TL;DR

- **CAS-quick: ~14 of 19 "failures" are MEASUREMENT artifacts, not tool failures.** The tool computed the right answer (often `Verified: TRUE`) but the **benchmark grader** failed to extract/match the model's verbose LaTeX / multi-value answer — or the ground truth itself is wrong. Only **4** are genuine tool-output issues (series `order_size`), and **Tier 1 already fixes those 4**.
- **olympiad-quick: 0 tool-loss, 0 tier-fixable, 40/49 no-tool-call.** Overwhelmingly **incapable** — proof / symbolic-in-n / "Giac can't brute-force" problems. Tooling cannot help; confirms the standing conclusion.
- **Recommendation:** Stop adding production CAS tooling. The only remaining addressable lever with real signal is **benchmark grader/extraction robustness** (a *measurement* fix) — it would re-score many CAS "failures" as correct, revealing the tool's true accuracy is well above the measured ~70%. Plus minor **dataset hygiene** (at least one bad ground truth). Olympiad needs a fundamentally different approach (out of scope).

## Recommendation

1. **Stop adding production CAS tooling.** CAS-quick's residual is dominated by *measurement* artifacts (the tool is already right ~14/19); olympiad is *capability* bound (incapable, no tool-loss). Neither rewards more tool plumbing. Tier 1/2/3 have harvested the real tool-output gains (the 4 series cases; plus Tier 2's escalation and Tier 3's unicode fixes that this pre-tier trace can't show).

2. **Highest-value remaining lever (measurement, not capability): benchmark grader/extraction robustness.** Make the grader match verbose LaTeX (`\(...\)`, `\int…=…+C`), multi-value answers (`3,1`), and known equivalences (`ln(abs(x))≡ln(x)`). This would re-score ~12 CAS "failures" as correct, revealing the tool's true accuracy is well above the measured ~70%. **Tier 2's `Verified: TRUE` signal is a natural input here** — a grader could trust a CAS-verified answer. This is the single change with real remaining signal.

3. **Dataset hygiene.** At least one ground truth is wrong (idx 42: det = 13, not -5). A quick audit of CAS-quick GTs is cheap and prevents permanent false-negatives.

4. **Measure, don't assume.** A small targeted re-run (CAS-quick + MATH L4/L5) on the *current* Tier-1/2/3 build would quantify how much the series/order_size fix and unicode/escalation work actually recovered — but the bucket shape above already says the ceiling on *tooling* is reached.

5. **Olympiad is out of scope for this stack** (confirmed): proof systems (Lean/Coq), olympiad-specific RAG, or a different model — not CAS plumbing.

## Caveats

- Traces predate Tier 1/2/3, so the `tool-loss`/series counts are the "before" picture; the 4 series cases are now fixed on `main`.
- Single-rater classification, controller-spot-checked on 4 CAS cases; the subagent's tier attribution was corrected downward after spot-check (an important reminder that "tool-loss" and "grader false-negative" look alike until you read the `Verified:` line and the raw result).
